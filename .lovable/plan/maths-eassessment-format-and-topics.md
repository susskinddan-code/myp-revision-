# MYP Maths eAssessment: format and topic list (from Dan's teacher notes, 2026-10-11)

Format: 2-hour on-screen exam, 100 marks, Standard or Extended, 6-10 tasks, four criteria (A-D), 25 marks per criterion.
Three task types: short answer (Task 1, about Q1-4, 7-10 marks each, criteria A and C), long real-life problem (Task 2, about 20 marks) plus a 10-mark question, investigation (Task 3, about 35 marks).
Tools on screen: graphing tools, tables, formula booklet.

Topics (use to check coverage of the Maths banks and papers):
1. Numerical and abstract reasoning - Number: absolute values, inequalities (compound, double), irrational numbers, surds, standard form, laws of exponents (integer and negative), number systems notation, direct and inverse proportion, number sequences. Algebra: factorising quadratics, solving quadratics, changing the subject.
2. Thinking with models - mappings, function notation, linear functions, y = mx + c, parallel and perpendicular lines, systems of equations, quadratic functions, algorithms.
3. Spatial reasoning - Geometry: metric conversions, volume of regular polyhedra, similarity and congruence, coordinate geometry (distance, midpoint, gradient), isometric transformations, enlargements, tessellations, gradients and intercepts, parallel lines, circle geometry, rotation about a point. Trigonometry: triangle properties, bearings, Pythagoras, right-angled ratios, sine rule, area = 1/2 ab sin C.
4. Reasoning with data - sampling, misinterpretation, graphs (bivariate, scatter, box plots, cumulative frequency), lines of best fit, quartiles and percentiles, IQR, correlation, relative frequency, response rates, sets (up to three), Venn diagrams, tree diagrams, sample spaces, mutually exclusive events, conditional probability.

Update: the fuller Standard/Extended lists (second upload) include the sine rule AND cosine rule, so the cosine-rule content is in scope.

## Full Standard Maths list (second upload)
Number: standard form; integers, irrational numbers, squares and roots; factors, primes and prime factors; HCF and LCM; recurring decimals; surds; exponents and laws (integer and negative); number lines and simple, compound and double inequalities; ratios; time zones, clocks, timetables; direct and inverse proportion.
Algebra: operating with expressions; forming, transposing and solving equations; substitution; expanding; factorising (algebraic and quadratic); solving quadratics; using formulae; flowcharts and simple algorithms; changing the subject; arithmetic and geometric sequences.
Functions and models: mappings, function notation, domain and range, linear functions, parallel and perpendicular lines, simultaneous equations, quadratic functions and their transformations, rational functions, shapes of more complex functions, graphing trig functions, linear programming with inequalities, algorithms. Networks: edges, arcs, nodes, paths, calculating pathways, weighted networks.
Geometry: classifying shapes and angles, angle properties, parallel lines and transversals, perimeter/area/volume, surface area and nets, coordinates, symmetry and reflection, metric conversions, volume of regular polyhedra and additional shapes, similarity and congruence, coordinate geometry, transformations, enlargement (rational factor, given point), tessellations, y = mx + c, perpendicular gradients, circle geometry, rotation.
Trigonometry: triangle properties, bearings, Pythagoras and its converse, right-angled ratios, sine and cosine rules with applications.
Statistics: discrete data, data collection and surveys, pie/bar/stem-and-leaf/pictograms, bivariate and scatter graphs, box plots, cumulative frequency, histograms for fixed intervals, lines of best fit, mean/mode/median for discrete and grouped data, quartiles and percentiles, range, IQR and standard deviation, correlation, limitations of statistical enquiry.
Probability: relative frequency, simple events, sample spaces, probability scale, theoretical vs experimental, response rates, sets (up to three), Venn and tree diagrams, mutually exclusive events, combined events, addition and multiplication rules, conditional probability, dependent and independent events.
Extended adds: number systems notation, number operations, number sequences, logarithms and their laws, upper and lower bounds (plus the above).

## Physics
Forces and energy: measurement, states of matter, kinetic theory, density, forces and motion, speed and motion graphs, Newton's laws, pressure, work, power, efficiency, gravity and gravitational fields, energy sources and environmental impact, energy transfer and conservation.
Electromagnetism: magnetism, electric and magnetic fields, static electricity, induction, AC/DC, current/voltage/power, generation and transmission, circuits.
Heat, light and sound: thermal physics, heat transfer, condensation and evaporation. Waves: longitudinal/transverse, sound, reflection, refraction, diffraction, wave equation, electromagnetic spectrum and applications.
Atomic physics: atomic structure, radioactivity, decay and half-life, forms of radiation, uses and dangers. Astrophysics: solar system, planets and satellites, Big Bang.

## Language and Literature blueprint (teacher notes)
Task 1: text and multimodal text, criteria A and B, including compare and contrast of two texts. Task 2: literary text, criteria C and D. Task 3: non-literary text, criteria B, C and D. Text types for Task 3 include informal email/letter, diary entry, article, blog (plus speeches, letters, interviews, proposals per the structures handout). Themes: identity, communities/migration, media, childhood and adolescence, relationships, power and protest, health and environment, social roles, utopias/dystopias, faith and values, betrayal and forgiveness.

## Language Acquisition (Emergent and Capable)
Objectives A listening, B reading, D writing. Unit 1 Identity and culture (self/family/friends, free time, food and health, festivals). Unit 2 Education and work (school life, future plans, jobs). Unit 3 The world we live in (weather, environment, global issues). Unit 4 Local area, travel and tourism (home, local area, holidays, culture and communities). Unit 5 Technology and media (mobile technology, social media, ...).
